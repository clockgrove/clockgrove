# Clockgrove Brand

**Status:** Canonical brand foundation  
**Applies to:** Clockgrove's product, documentation, platform demos, web presence, community and brand art, including generated and AI-assisted work on them  
**Does not apply to:** a world's own art and creative direction, including AI-assisted asset creation for its pack. A world's art follows that world's style bible in its own repository, in [Assetforge's](https://github.com/clockgrove/assetforge) bible format (Raccoon World: [clockgrove/raccoon-world#2](https://github.com/clockgrove/raccoon-world/issues/2)).

---

## 1. Brand idea

Clockgrove is a **living illustrated world**.

It is a place where time passes, weather changes, neighborhoods remember, creatures form habits and relationships, objects have histories, and the world keeps going when nobody is watching.

The brand should make advanced simulation and agent technology feel **warm, legible, playful, and alive** rather than futuristic, sterile, robotic, or abstract.

Clockgrove is not visually about AI.

It is visually about **a world worth caring about**.

The technology should disappear behind a charming, bustling place full of small causes and consequences.

---

## 2. Core promise

> A persistent world where everything has a place, a history, and a point of view.

Clockgrove lets people observe, converse with, understand, influence, and eventually author living worlds populated by durable actors.

Clockgrove is the platform for independently authored worlds. Each world supplies its own
inhabitants, setting and presentation. The [reference world's creative guidance](https://github.com/clockgrove/raccoon-world/blob/main/README.md)
lives with that world; this document describes the platform brand.

---

## 3. Emotional territory

Clockgrove lives at the intersection of:

- **cozy curiosity** — the pleasure of noticing what everyone is doing;
- **busy neighborhood life** — many small stories happening at once;
- **gentle mischief** — animals have motives, habits, rivalries, shortcuts, and schemes;
- **living systems** — rain matters, time matters, food moves, things grow, relationships change;
- **inspectable magic** — surprising behavior can be explained rather than hand-waved;
- **creative possibility** — the world can be extended, authored, forked, and reshaped;
- **earned sentimentality** — affection comes from persistent history and recognizable behavior, not forced cuteness.

The target feeling is:

> “I want to zoom in and see what everyone is up to.”

---

## 4. Brand pillars

### 4.1 The world is alive

Clockgrove is never a static scene.

Every image, demo, and description should suggest activity before, during, and after the depicted moment. Someone is carrying something. Someone is headed somewhere. Rain is filling a puddle. A cart is arriving. A squirrel is balancing a stack of parcels. An inhabitant is peering into a bin. A bird is posting a notice. A fox shopkeeper is opening an awning.

The image should imply **systems in motion**.

### 4.2 Everything belongs somewhere

Spaces matter. Homes, burrows, porches, shops, gardens, sheds, trees, bridges, alleys, paths, markets, ponds, rooftops, and rooms should feel functional and inhabited.

Objects should have owners, uses, histories, and destinations.

The world feels coherent because things are not merely decorative; they appear to participate in everyday life.

### 4.3 Characters are understandable, not generic mascots

Characters should have recognizable jobs, routines, relationships, preferences, and immediate intentions.

An inhabitant should not just stand smiling at the viewer. It should be dragging a grocery sack, checking a map, carrying kits across a puddle, inspecting a broken wheel, sneaking a snack, talking to a neighbor, or hurrying home before the rain gets worse.

Clockgrove characters are **actors inside a world**, not brand mascots posing outside it.

### 4.4 Complexity should feel friendly

Underneath Clockgrove are event sourcing, persistent actors, cognition, memory, simulation, tools, timelines, and causal traces.

The brand translates that complexity into intuitive metaphors:

- neighborhoods rather than clusters;
- inhabitants rather than processes;
- histories rather than logs;
- conversations rather than query interfaces;
- paths and places rather than coordinate systems;
- routines and memories rather than state machines;
- changing weather and clocks rather than scheduler diagrams.

Technical material may be precise, but the public-facing brand should remain human-readable and welcoming.

### 4.5 The future is not chrome

Clockgrove must avoid the default visual language of AI products:

- no glowing brains;
- no circuit-board motifs;
- no neon cyberpunk grids;
- no humanoid robots;
- no floating holograms as a shorthand for intelligence;
- no generic blue-purple AI gradients;
- no faceless synthetic assistants.

Clockgrove's future is expressed through **behavior**, not futuristic props.

---

## 5. World population rule

Clockgrove worlds are populated by **anthropomorphic and natural animals, creatures, plants, places, and objects**.

### Absolute visual rule

**Do not depict humans. Do not depict robots.**

This applies to hero art, product screenshots, world concepts, promotional illustrations, diagrams intended to represent the world, and generated creative assets unless a future explicitly approved world pack changes the rule.

Human users may be represented indirectly through an in-world persona such as the Caretaker, a cursor, a nameplate, a letter, an inventory, a point of view, or another world-specific abstraction.

The absence of humans helps Clockgrove feel like its own coherent place rather than a conventional game world populated by player avatars.

The absence of robots prevents the visual world from collapsing into generic “AI” iconography.

---

## 6. World character language

Each world owns its character identities, species, relationships and visual language. Platform
illustrations should show inhabitants with readable intentions without making one world’s cast
part of the Clockgrove identity. World-specific guidance belongs in the world repository.

---

## 7. Relationship to the busy animal-town picture-book reference

The creative reference is the **busy illustrated children's-town feeling** of mid-century busy animal-town picture books: dense but readable scenes, anthropomorphic animal residents, tiny simultaneous stories, charming practical vehicles and buildings, visual jokes, and the pleasure of discovering new details every time you look.

Clockgrove should use that reference as a **design principle, not a reproduction target**.

Translate the spirit into Clockgrove by emphasizing:

- many concurrent activities;
- readable cause and effect;
- functional places and vehicles;
- animals doing ordinary, specific things;
- labels, signs, maps, tools, parcels, food, carts, ladders, umbrellas, wagons, and household objects;
- visual storytelling that rewards inspection;
- warmth and humor without chaos becoming illegible.

Then add the defining Clockgrove layer:

> Every little activity belongs to a persistent simulation and could have happened for a reason.

The viewer should be able to imagine clicking any inhabitant or object and asking what it is doing, where it came from, what happened earlier, or why something changed.

---

## 8. Dynamic-world storytelling

Clockgrove imagery should not merely show a cute town. It should suggest **state, history, and causality**.

Good scenes contain multiple visible chains such as:

- dark clouds → rain → puddles → umbrellas → wet footprints → sheltered food stall;
- tipped trash bin → scattered snacks → investigating inhabitant → annoyed crow → neighbor carrying a broom;
- fallen branch → blocked path → detour sign → cart taking another route;
- growing vegetable patch → harvested basket → market stall → family dinner preparation;
- missing wheel → broken cart → mechanic squirrel → delayed delivery;
- parent → kits following → one distracted kit → sibling looking back;
- porch feeder → regular visitors → empty bowl → caretaker note or refill activity.

Each scene should imply that the world has a **before** and an **after**.

---

## 9. Brand voice

Clockgrove's written voice is:

- curious;
- clear;
- quietly playful;
- technically grounded when needed;
- warm without becoming twee;
- confident without sounding corporate;
- imaginative without pretending uncertainty is fact.

Prefer concrete language over AI jargon.

### Prefer

- “The world keeps going while you're away.”
- “Ask an inhabitant why they went to the porch.”
- “See what changed overnight.”
- “Every actor has a history.”
- “Fork the world and see what happens.”
- “Talk to anything.”

### Avoid

- “Unlock next-generation AI experiences.”
- “Revolutionary multi-agent orchestration.”
- “AI-powered immersive ecosystem.”
- “Leverage cutting-edge autonomous intelligence.”

The technology is real and sophisticated. The brand does not need to announce that sophistication in every sentence.

---

## 10. Naming language

Clockgrove vocabulary should feel like a living place rather than an enterprise platform.

Preferred terms include:

- world;
- neighborhood;
- place;
- room;
- actor;
- inhabitant;
- caretaker;
- memory;
- history;
- journal;
- timeline;
- branch;
- skill;
- routine;
- event;
- weather;
- relationship;
- world pack;
- authoring;
- inspect;
- why.

Use technical terms such as Durable Object, PostgreSQL, MCP, event sourcing, or model routing in technical contexts, not as primary consumer-facing brand language.

---

## 11. Brand test

A Clockgrove artifact is on-brand when it makes someone feel at least three of these things:

1. **There is more happening here than I noticed at first.**
2. **These inhabitants seem to have lives of their own.**
3. **I can tell why parts of the scene look the way they do.**
4. **I want to inspect or talk to something.**
5. **This feels cozy and playful without feeling childish or disposable.**
6. **The world seems persistent beyond the frame.**
7. **The technology feels powerful without visually shouting “AI.”**

If the primary impression is “generic AI startup,” “mobile game splash screen,” “Disney-like character poster,” “cyberpunk simulation,” or “a set of mascots posing for a camera,” the work is off-brand.

---

## 12. Short brand brief for generative systems

When a model needs a compact description of Clockgrove, use:

> Clockgrove is a warm, persistent illustrated world filled with anthropomorphic animal inhabitants living busy everyday lives. Think of the density, charm, visual storytelling, practical little buildings and vehicles, and simultaneous neighborhood activity of a classic busy children's town illustration, translated into a modern dynamic simulation. Every character should appear to be doing something for a reason; weather, time, objects, paths, relationships, and prior events visibly affect the scene. Cozy Pacific Northwest naturalism, gentle humor, readable detail, expressive animals, no humans, no robots, no futuristic AI imagery. The world should feel clickable, inspectable, conversational, and alive beyond the current frame.
